# OS-software-Design

	•	Briefly summarize The Gaming Room client and their software requirements. Who was the client? What type of software did they want you to design?
	•	The client, The Gaming Room, has a successful Android application game. They are looking to expand into web-based browser formats to reach a larger customer base. The software required should be able to support many users on at the same time, playing many different matches, in real-time.
	•	What did you do particularly well in developing this documentation?
	•	In particular, I worked through the technical evaluation for Linux OS and how it could benefit the web application. The different advantages and disadvantages were key to be able to select the appropriate OS for our server environment to ensure that the business can continue to grow with minimal overhead as desired.
	•	What about the process of working through a design document did you find helpful when developing the code?
	•	The process of working through the document helps me with my frame of thinking when developing the code. I laid out my thoughts and used the document as a reference, which keeps me organized throughout the process. 
	•	If you could choose one part of your work on these documents to revise, what would you pick? How would you improve it?
	•	I would like to work through the alternative OS comparison to make them more fluid and readable. My formatting is clunky and doesn’t inspire me when I look through the documentation.
	•	How did you interpret the user’s needs and implement them into your software design? Why is it so important to consider the user’s needs when designing?
	•	My design document kept me focused, by analyzing the key business needs for The Gaming Room. The client needed to have a web application that would available to all browsers on many different platforms, ran smoothly with a multitude of users, and was easy to update/add features. We ensured that the software had access to development tools such as containerization and a fluid server architecture. If the user’s needs aren’t met, you would have to rework it until satisfactory. We should strive to fulfill the clients needs to help them with their goals (i.e. scalability, reliability, usability).
	•	How did you approach designing software? What techniques or strategies would you use in the future to analyze and design a similar software application?
	•	My approach mirrors that of the SLDC, pivoting as necessary depending on client or specific project. I look to analyze the client’s needs, identify areas of concern, plan for the architecture, develop supporting components, and find solutions to problems using clear documentation. UML diagrams are incredibly intuitive and useful for the interactions throughout an application.
